# Post-press finishing research for a top-down carton renderer

Phase 1 research, gathered 2026-10-17. Sources are trade handbooks (Iggesund), finishing suppliers, printer guides (English and Korean) and patents. Every factual statement links to a page that was actually fetched. Statements marked **[search-summary]** come from a search-engine summary of the page; the page itself could not be fetched (403 or redirect). Statements marked **[inference]** are optical reasoning by the author and are not sourced.

Note on method: the pages were read as text. Images were not viewed, because the Chrome extension was not connected and the brief forbids downloading images. Visual claims therefore come from what pages say in words. See `visual-reference-library.md` for what this leaves open.

## 0. Cross-cutting optics (applies to every process)

- **Specular vs diffuse.** A gloss surface reflects one mirror-like lobe, so the eye sees brightness only where the light source, or a bright part of the room, sits at the mirror angle. A matte or rough surface scatters light in all directions and looks about equally bright from every angle **[inference]**. Iggesund says gloss varnish is "intended to reflect light in a uniform way", that the result depends on a smooth substrate, and that "the human eye is very sensitive to contrast" between gloss and matt areas ([Iggesund Graphics Handbook, full PDF](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh---full-copy---en/0_graphics_handbook_en.pdf), Varnishing chapter).
- **Metal foil behaves like a mirror.** 4OVER4 describes foil as "a mirror sitting slightly below the surface of the sheet": it flashes bright when it faces a light and "looks dark when it doesn't face a light source" ([4OVER4 gold & silver foil guide](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/guide/gold-and-silver-foil-printing-explained.md)).
- **Overhead photography of foil (the case this app renders).** A studio photographer shot a gold-foil card from directly above with one window light. Without a reflector the foil "looked dark and didn't shine", because it mirrored the dark surroundings of the camera. Holding a white foam-core board beside the camera made it look bright. The final overhead image was a Photoshop composite ([tog.ink foil photography](https://tog.ink/blog/?p=2091)). A forum user also reports that direct on-camera flash makes gold foil "look black" ([TalkPhotography thread, search-summary](https://www.talkphotography.co.uk/threads/product-photography-glass-item-with-metallic-gold-foil.671537/)).
- **Gloss measurement.** Paper gloss is normally measured at 75° (TAPPI T480 / ISO 8254-1). For high-gloss varnishes ISO recommends 20°. ASTM D523 uses 60° and suggests 85° when the 60° reading is below 10 ([HunterLab note via search-summary](https://support.hunterlab.com/hc/en-us/article_attachments/201362279); [ISO 8254-1 listing](https://www.sis.se/produkter/pappersteknik-d9ce2e65/papper-och-papp/sseniso825412009/)). No authoritative table of typical GU values per finish was found. A PrintPlanet thread notes that no such list exists ([PrintPlanet "Paper Gloss Levels"](https://printplanet.com/threads/paper-gloss-levels.264542/)). **Gap: GU values below are sparse.**

## A. Foil stamping (박)

### A.1 Manufacturing method
- **Hot foil stamping.** Foil arrives on a heat-resistant carrier film. A heated die (Iggesund calls it a "matrix") presses the foil against the board, releases it from the carrier and bonds it. The key process variables are foil type, dwell time (s), pressure (bar) and temperature (°C) ([Iggesund, Hot foil stamping in practice](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh-pdf----en/hot_foil_stamping_in_practice.pdf); same text in the full handbook).
- **Layer stacks** (Iggesund diagram):
  - *Metallic foil:* carrier film / release layer / lacquer (carries the colour, e.g. gold tint) / aluminium / adhesion layer.
  - *Pigment foil:* carrier film / release layer / pigment / adhesion layer. **There is no aluminium layer.**
  ([Iggesund handbook](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh---full-copy---en/0_graphics_handbook_en.pdf), p. 28.)
- **Typical layer thicknesses** (patent literature, so treat as ranges): PET carrier 12–50 µm; release layer about 0.025–1 µm (another source gives 0.1–2 µm); vacuum-metallised aluminium about 20–100 nm (one worked example uses 50 nm); lacquer about 0.5–3 µm (another source gives 1–10 µm); adhesive about 1–4 µm ([US5731064 stamping-foil patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5731064); [US4275116](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4275116); [US6514586](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6514586), all via search-summary). **[inference]** The layer that transfers is only a few µm thick, so flat hot foil adds almost no height. Any relief you see comes from the die pressing into the board.
- **Hot vs cold foil.**
  - Cold foil is applied inline on the press: an adhesive is printed, then the foil is laminated to it and the carrier is peeled away. It needs a smooth, flat substrate, it cannot be combined with embossing, and it "will lay completely flat". Hot foil has "a very slight raised appearance", is "traditionally… more brilliant", works on rough or textured stock and combines with embossing ([Resource Label: cold vs hot foil](https://www.resourcelabel.com/blog/2022/05/21/cold-foil-vs-hot-foil-for-labels/), search-summary).
  - Cold foil can be overprinted with ink to make colours, for example transparent gold ink over silver cold foil on Diamond Packaging's Pierre Cardin Choc carton ([Diamond Packaging award article](https://diamondpackaging.com/articles/187/diamond-packaging-wins-three-awards-in-24th-annual-fsea-gold-leaf-awards-competition)). Iggesund also suggests "a foil with offset print on top of it" for "an astonishingly bright result" ([Iggesund handbook](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh---full-copy---en/0_graphics_handbook_en.pdf), p. 18).
- **Foil embossing** (foil and relief in one stroke) uses a combination die "with a foil breakage edge". The relief depth in this case is **0.25–0.60 mm** ([Iggesund, Embossing and debossing in practice](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh-pdf----en/embossing_and_debossing_in_practice.pdf), p. 65).

### A.3 Substrate interaction
- Foil magnifies substrate defects: "any flaws are strongly accentuated when foiled" ([Iggesund](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh---full-copy---en/0_graphics_handbook_en.pdf), p. 19). **[inference]** On uncoated or textured stock the foil follows the fibre topography, so the mirror breaks into a grainy, sparkly reflection. On textured stock, foil "lands on the raised peaks and skips the valleys" ([4OVER4 holographic](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/holographic-foil-stamping-explained-effects-costs-and-artwork-setup)).
- **Foil over lamination.** Some foils are made for it: API's general metallic grade is described as suitable for "coated paper and matt lamination" ([API Foilmakers summary, search-summary](https://www.labelsandlabeling.com/news/new-products/api-foils-launches-metallic-foil-and-holographic-decorative-products)), and KURZ has a Luxor/Alufin MTU grade for UV inks and coatings ([Printweek / KURZ, search-summary](https://printweek.com/content/event/kurz-boasts-the-uses-of-its-luxoralufin-foil)). A 2022 KMUTT study nonetheless found matte-laminated art card "poor in foil transfer and adhesion" compared with plain art card ([KMUTT publication, search-summary](https://kirim.kmutt.ac.th/converis/portal/detail/Publication/1034155865?lang=en_GB)). Iggesund notes that thermal films can cause "problem[s] when hot foil stamping" ([Iggesund](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh---full-copy---en/0_graphics_handbook_en.pdf), lamination adhesive table).
- **Foil covers the print.** If foil and print overlap, the foil hides the print ([Photomon](https://biz.photomon.com/products/106)). Foil is kiss-fit (butted) to print, not trapped ([Kluge](https://www.kluge.biz/tips/press-tip-primary-design-considerations/)).

### A.4 Numbers summary (foil)
- Transferred foil layer: a few µm; aluminium about 20–100 nm (patents above).
- Foil-emboss depth: 0.25–0.60 mm (Iggesund).
- No gloss-unit data was found for foils. **Gap.**

## B. Embossing (볼록형압 / 양각) and debossing (오목형압 / 음각)

### B.1 Method
- A stamp (male die) and a make-ready or counter-die with the mirror image press the board at high pressure, sometimes with heat. "A relief pattern can be **0.15 to 2.5 mm** high or deep." Shallow level differences allow faster machine speeds and easier stacking ([Iggesund embossing PDF](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh-pdf----en/embossing_and_debossing_in_practice.pdf)).
- **Die materials and configurations** (Iggesund table): brass, engraved, multi-level, most durable; copper, etched, mostly single level; magnesium, engraved, limited multi-level; magnesium, etched, single level only. The configurations drawn are one level, two levels, many levels, impressed, one level rounded, and sculptural (same source). 4OVER4 lists single-level (straight walls, flat plateau), bevelled (angled walls), multi-level (stepped plateaus, brass) and sculpted (hand-tooled contours, no flat top) ([4OVER4 embossing guide](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/embossed-printing-explained)). Hinderer+Mühlich lists relief types round, prismatic, flat, single-level, multi-level and sculptured, each as emboss or deboss ([H+M embossing dies](https://hinderer-muehlich.com/en/stamping-tools-top-quality-products-for-the-graphics-industry/stamping-dies-counter-dies-set-up-systems-make-ready-material/hot-foil-stamping-dies-embossing-dies-cnc-engraved/embossing-dies-for-impressive-haptic-effects)).
- **Blind vs registered.** Blind uses no ink or foil. Registered aligns to print, and 4OVER4 recommends making the printed shape "a fraction wider" than the emboss ([4OVER4](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/embossed-printing-explained)). Combination means foil plus emboss in one pass. Korean usage: 블라인드 형압 vs 인쇄 형압 ([Morningprint](https://www.morningprint.co.kr/finishing/open_finishing3.php)).
- **Process order.** Embossing is done last, after print, varnish and lamination. Foil embossing is the exception and happens simultaneously ([Iggesund](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh-pdf----en/embossing_and_debossing_in_practice.pdf)).
- **Reverse side.** An emboss pushes up from behind, so the back shows a recess. Morningprint warns that "뒷면에 형압 흔적이 생긴다" (the back shows the emboss) ([Morningprint](https://www.morningprint.co.kr/finishing/open_finishing3.php)). On a carton interior this is usually hidden, but on a flat top-down shot it is invisible anyway.

### B.2 Board constraints and cracking
- FBB is more compressible than SBB, so the make-ready must be compensated. Fully bleached multi-ply long-fibre board gives the best deep relief. "To avoid surface cracking, high elasticity of the coating is important" ([Iggesund](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh-pdf----en/embossing_and_debossing_in_practice.pdf)).
- Over-forming causes cracks, while under-forming blurs contours. Material thins on the flanks as the emboss path increases ([JPMTR Advances 48, 2022, search-summary](https://jpmtr.org/Advances_48_2022_07.pdf)).
- Gloss coating or laminate "can whiten or crack along the shoulder", and film laminate can stress-whiten at the edge ([4OVER4](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/embossed-printing-explained)). On deep emboss, a silk or soft-touch coating "may crack slightly along the impression edges" ([Print Peppermint](https://printpeppermint.com/blogs/graphic-design/embossing-vs-debossing-an-in-depth-guide)).
- Embossing over printed areas can strip colour, so it is better avoided or used with coated stock ([WowPress 형압](https://wowpress.co.kr/wow2.0/w_guide/doc/g4_2.html)).
- Recommended stock (US): 24 pt or thicker for light emboss, 32 pt or thicker for deep emboss or deboss ([Print Peppermint](https://printpeppermint.com/blogs/graphic-design/embossing-vs-debossing-an-in-depth-guide)). Korean guidance: 250 g/m² or more for 엠보싱/디보싱 ([search-summary of Korean vendor pages](https://boxmaster.co.kr/blog/%EC%9D%B8%EC%87%84%ED%95%99%EA%B0%9C%EB%A1%A0/%ED%8C%A8%ED%82%A4%EC%A7%80%EC%A0%9C%EC%9E%91-%ED%9B%84%EA%B0%80%EA%B3%B5%EC%8B%9C%EA%B0%81%EA%B3%BC-%EC%B4%89%EA%B0%81-%EB%AA%A8%EB%91%90-%EC%82%AC%EB%A1%9C%EC%9E%A1%EB%8A%94-%ED%98%95%EC%95%95)).

### B.3 Look and optics
- Embossing "catches light" and debossing "creates shadow" ([Print Peppermint](https://printpeppermint.com/blogs/graphic-design/embossing-vs-debossing-an-in-depth-guide)). Embossing is "most striking on light and glossy substrates" ([H+M](https://hinderer-muehlich.com/en/stamping-tools-top-quality-products-for-the-graphics-industry/stamping-dies-counter-dies-set-up-systems-make-ready-material/hot-foil-stamping-dies-embossing-dies-cnc-engraved/embossing-dies-for-impressive-haptic-effects)).
- **[inference]** On a diffuse board under an overhead soft key, a 0.15–0.6 mm relief is seen almost entirely through *shading of the sloped walls* (Lambertian falloff, N·L). Walls facing the key get slightly brighter and walls facing away get slightly darker. Cast shadows are tiny because the relief is shallow and the light is large. A flat plateau (single-level die) has the same brightness as the background, so only its outline reads. A rounded or sculpted die gives continuous gradients. On a glossy laminate or varnish the walls also *move the specular highlight*, so the edges flash. That makes emboss far more visible on gloss than on uncoated stock.

### B.4 Numbers
- Relief 0.15–2.5 mm; foil-emboss 0.25–0.60 mm ([Iggesund](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh-pdf----en/embossing_and_debossing_in_practice.pdf)).
- No published figure was found for bevel wall angle or shoulder radius. **Gap.**

## C. Lamination (라미네이팅) vs uncoated (비코팅)

### C.1 Method and materials
- A plastic film is glue-laminated to the board. BOPP (polypropylene) is the most common film for gloss and protection. Adhesive systems are water-based acrylic, solvent-free PU, thermal (pre-applied) and UV. Water-based acrylic gives "good creasing, embossing, hot foil stamping ability". Thermal films cause problems for hot foil, and UV adhesive carries a risk of cracking and curl ([Iggesund, Film and foil lamination in practice](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh---full-copy---en/0_graphics_handbook_en.pdf)).
- Film thickness: thermal BOPP gloss and matte films come in about 18–27 µm. EVA-coated films use a 12–15 µm BOPP base with EVA adhesive making up the rest. Lamination temperature is 100–130 °C ([Cosmo Films datasheet and Arrsys listing, search-summary](https://arrsys.com/product/bopp-thermal-lamination-film/)). A 30 µm soft-touch film is listed as 15 µm OPP + 12 µm EVA + 3 µm soft-touch layer ([MyBinding / Arrsys, search-summary](https://arrsys.com/?p=18006)).
- Gloss values (sparse): one matte thermal film quotes 45° gloss below 10 (Gardner D-2457) ([Cosmo Films, search-summary](https://cosmofilms.com/public/uploads/pdf/1708539536.pdf); not readable on direct fetch). Soft-feel coatings in patents target 60° gloss of 20 GU or less, or 4–12 GU. Conventional extruded matte films are quoted at 8–12 % and PU soft films below 5 % ([patents via search-summary](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20150132521); [EP2399741](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.2/patents/EP2399741NWA1/document.html)).

### C.2 Appearance and why
- **Gloss laminate.** A smooth, transparent PP surface gives a strong mirror reflection, and colours look more saturated ("glass-like") ([GoGoPrint matte vs gloss, search-summary](https://gogoprint.com.my/blog/ggp-matte-vs-gloss-lamination-my)). Any board irregularity is highlighted ([Iggesund](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh---full-copy---en/0_graphics_handbook_en.pdf)).
- **Matte laminate.** The surface is micro-roughened, so the reflection spreads into a broad, low sheen **[inference]**. Vendors disagree on the colour effect: some say colours look "slightly deeper and richer", others "more subdued" ([PakFactory / Carepac via search-summary](https://pakfactory.com/blog/matte-vs-gloss-lamination/)). It is more prone to scuffs and to showing fingerprints on dark solids ([Friesens lamination, search-summary](https://www.friesens.com/yearbooks/customizations/yearbook-cover-options/lamination-yb/)).
- **Soft-touch (velvet).** Matte with a velvety feel; "Dark colors can look richer… bright neons can appear a touch muted" ([4OVER4 FAQ, search-summary](https://www.4over4.com/faqs/general/is-soft-touch-lamination-the-same-as-matte-lamination)). It has a lower gloss than standard matte (see the GU numbers above).
- **How matte film differs from uncoated paper** (key for the renderer):
  1. Under a matte film the ink still sits on a sealed, coated surface, so *solid density stays high*. Uncoated paper absorbs ink, giving lower solid density, more dot gain and lower contrast ([PIWorld coated vs uncoated, search-summary](https://www.piworld.com/choosing-inks-for-color-printing-coated-vs-uncoated); [PrintPlanet GRACoL & uncoated](https://printplanet.com/threads/gracol-and-uncoated.4791/)). Optical dot gain from lateral light scatter inside the paper is larger on uncoated stock ([Namedanian et al., SPIE 2014, search-summary](https://proceedings.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/9015/901509/Physical-and-optical-dot-gain--characterization-and-relation-to/10.1117/12.2035713.full)).
  2. **[inference]** Matte film is a continuous dielectric layer. Its roughness is a fine micro-texture with an even sheen and no fibres, and it adds a faint, low-contrast veil of surface reflection over dark colours. Uncoated paper has visible fibre or felt texture at macro scale, a slightly warmer and duller print, and very little sheen.
  3. Spot UV cannot go on uncoated stock: "the varnish bleeds into the porous paper" ([Flexpress](https://www.flexpress.co.uk/spot-uv-guide/)).
- Metallised film lamination gives "more of a metallic look than… a metallic ink" ([Iggesund](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh---full-copy---en/0_graphics_handbook_en.pdf)).
- Korean vendors describe 라미네이팅 (film) and UV코팅 (liquid) as both coming in 유광 and 무광: "유광: 깨끗하고 선명한 느낌 / 무광: 은은하고 고급스러운 느낌" (gloss: clean and vivid; matt: subtle and premium) ([WowPress 코팅](https://wowpress.co.kr/wow2.0/w_guide/doc/g4_3.html)).

## D. Liquid coatings: spot UV, flood UV, varnish

### D.1 Methods and chemistries
- Varnishes are inks without pigment. *Overprint (oil) varnish* dries by absorption and oxidation, gives "high gloss" and carries a risk of yellowing. *Dispersion (aqueous)* is "very high gloss" and quick-drying. *UV varnish* gives the "highest gloss" and the best resistance, and "the gloss depends on the varnish thickness and viscosity". All three are also available in matt versions. Application is inline (plate and blanket, coater) or offline ([Iggesund, Varnishing in practice](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh---full-copy---en/0_graphics_handbook_en.pdf)). Finishes available include glossy, satin (semi-gloss), pearlised and matt (same source).
- Aqueous coating is harder to spot-apply, while UV suits spot work ([John S. Byrne / Royle coatings, search-summary](https://johnsbyrne.com/blog/printing-packaging-terms-know-part-2-coatings-varnishes/)). Satin sits between matte and gloss (same source).

### D.2 Thickness numbers
- Standard spot UV is "only a couple of microns thick", or under 10 µm at other printers. Raised spot UV is usually 20–80 µm, with a recommended maximum of 80 µm and no more than 20 µm for fine text ([Flexpress Spot UV guide](https://www.flexpress.co.uk/spot-uv-guide/)).
- Friesens offers three levels: **10 µm** standard, **50 µm** tactile, and **100 µm** maximum in one pass ([Friesens Spot Gloss UV](https://www.friesens.com/books/specialty-options/specialty-finishes/spotuv/)).
- QinPrinting quotes 10–20 µm ([QinPrinting spot UV](https://www.qinprinting.com/spot-uv/)).
- Digital inkjet coaters (MGI JETvarnish 3D) range from 3 µm flat to 100 µm raised, or 200 µm with the "Twin" option ([MGI JV3D brochure, search-summary](https://kmbs.konicaminolta.us/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Konica_Minolta_MGI_JV3D_Brochure.pdf)). Duplo DDC coaters cover 10–80 µm ([Duplo, search-summary](https://www.duplousa.com/2025/02/quick-guide-creating-the-spot-uv-layer/)).
- **Raised/3D UV is a separate future option.** It behaves like a low dome (see E) with a sharp printed edge.

### D.3 Appearance and why
- Spot UV over matte or soft-touch lamination is the standard high-contrast pairing ([Flexpress](https://www.flexpress.co.uk/spot-uv-guide/); [QinPrinting](https://www.qinprinting.com/spot-uv/)). Gloss laminate or gloss substrate is "not compatible" because the sheens compete ([Flexpress](https://www.flexpress.co.uk/spot-uv-guide/)). A Korean comic printer says the same: with 부분 UV the cover must be 무광 coated, and gloss over gloss loses the effect ([icomics FAQ, search-summary](https://www.icomics.co.kr/bbs/board.php?bo_table=su106&wr_id=16&list_style=list)).
- "On dark matte stock, a logo can appear to shift between visible and nearly invisible as the viewing angle changes" ([QinPrinting](https://www.qinprinting.com/spot-uv/)). Friesens: "photos don't do spot gloss UV justice", and very fine lines show little contrast ([Friesens](https://www.friesens.com/books/specialty-options/specialty-finishes/spotuv/)).
- A gloss varnish "makes colour images look more saturated" ([Iggesund](https://www.iggesund.com/globalassets/iggesund/services/knowledge/iam/graphics-handbook/gh---full-copy---en/0_graphics_handbook_en.pdf)).
- **[inference]** UV varnish is a clear film (refractive index about 1.5), so it is *transparent over* the print. Its effects are (a) a strong specular lobe, (b) slightly deeper colour, because the matte layer's surface scatter that lifts blacks is removed, and (c) at 10 µm, no visible relief at top-down camera distance. In a top-down photo with a soft overhead key, the spot UV only flashes where the mirror angle catches the key or a bright environment. Elsewhere it reads as slightly darker and richer than the surrounding matte.

## E. Epoxy / resin doming (에폭시, 도밍)

### E.1 Method
- Liquid resin is dispensed onto a pre-cut part. It "spreads until it reaches the sharp edge" and is held there by surface tension (the "cliff effect"), so the dome takes the outline of the part ([Incure resin doming guide](https://incurelab.com/wp/resin-doming-the-ultimate-guide); [Screen Printing mag doming article](https://screenprintingmag.com/?p=128)).
- Starting dose is 0.15–0.20 g/cm². Too much resin overflows the edge and too little fails to reach it. Plotter-cut edges are more consistent than vertical die-cuts ([Screen Printing mag](https://screenprintingmag.com/?p=128)).
- Defects: *tunnelling* (resin pulls back from the edge on low-surface-energy substrates) and trapped bubbles ([Incure](https://incurelab.com/wp/resin-doming-the-ultimate-guide)).

### E.2 Epoxy vs polyurethane
- Epoxy and one-part UV PU cure fast but yellow and degrade outdoors. Two-part PU has the largest market share, weathers better, resists yellowing and is "self-healing" ([Screen Printing mag](https://screenprintingmag.com/?p=128)). Epoxy is harder and more abrasion-resistant, while PU is flexible ([Incure, search-summary](https://incurelab.com/wp/doming-resins-a-professionals-guide-to-high-quality-labels-and-badges)). Hardness: flexible grades Shore A 60–80, rigid grades Shore D 70–85. Viscosity 450–1,500 cP ([Incure](https://incurelab.com/wp/resin-doming-the-ultimate-guide)).
- **Korean terminology trap.** In Korean print shops "에폭시" on business cards often means *digital raised UV (Scodix-type) built up in layers*, not poured PU. Photomon's 디지털금박/에폭시 layer must be a spot colour literally named "scodix", and is built by "투명한 수지를 여러 번 덧올려" (clear resin laid on several times) ([Photomon 디지털금박](https://biz.photomon.com/products/109?op1=3&op2=115&op3=10&op4=131&op5=15&op6=146)). True poured domes are sold as 에폭시 스티커 ([Redprinting EPX](https://www.redprinting.co.kr/ko/landing/EPX); [Publog 에폭시 스티커](https://www.publog.co.kr/blog/epoxy-sticker-guide)). The app should label these two separately.

### E.3 Dimensions
- Dome height is typically about 1.5 mm, with production variation up to about 0.5 mm ([Stickers International](https://www.stickersinternational.co.uk/products/domed-stickers/)). Incure gives 1.5–2.0 mm ([Incure](https://incurelab.com/wp/resin-doming-the-ultimate-guide)). Dometag gives 1.5–1.8 mm (±0.6 mm), with smaller domes running thicker; James Labels gives 2–3 mm ([search-summary of Dometag and James Labels](https://dometag.com/help/dome)).

### E.4 Optics
- The dome "acts as a lens that enhances the colors and depth of the underlying graphics" ([Incure](https://incurelab.com/wp/resin-doming-the-ultimate-guide)). No quantified magnification for labels was found. Plano-convex dome magnifiers give about 1.5–3× ([Specialty Vision, search-summary](https://specialty.vision/article/dome-magnifiers/)), but those are hemispheres, far steeper than a 1.5 mm dome on a 20 mm label.
- **[inference]** A 1.5 mm dome on a 20–30 mm part is nearly flat in the middle and steep at the meniscus edge. Expect:
  - Near the centre: almost no magnification, but a crisp specular highlight of the key light (a small, sharp, bright "window" reflection). Top-down, the highlight sits close to the centre for an overhead key.
  - Within about 1–3 mm of the edge: strong surface curvature. This bends the image of the print inward or outward (edge distortion and compression), shows a bright rim or ring highlight from the light around the camera, and a thin dark line where total internal reflection and the steep slope send camera rays elsewhere.
  - Clear and glossy overall, with no texture.
